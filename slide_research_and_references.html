Gap Analysis

Existing product Gap
IMD / MOSDAC / WebGIS Portals Coarse regional forecast cones and PDF bulletins; no localized 200m building risk grid or 3D digital twin.
JTWC & Global Track Hubs Meteorological track & wind radii only; no critical infrastructure exposure or highway passability simulation.
NOAA SLOSH & Global Flood Tools Coarse coastal scale (~1-5 km); lacks astronomical tide coupling & overland culvert drainage overtopping pathways.
Standard Disaster Dashboards Post-disaster reporting only; no anticipatory decision intelligence or automated multilingual advisory TTS broadcast.
The Gap CycloneX Fills
Every listed tool does hazard visualization OR meteorological tracking OR post-event damage reporting — none of them connects the entire causal resilience pathway from storm telemetry ➔ GEE multi-sensor satellite layers ➔ physics surge & runoff models ➔ 200m building risk grid ➔ Gemini 3.7 Flash decision intelligence with automated NDMA IAP generation and interactive virtual countermeasures inside a single install-free browser workspace.

Research Literature

1. Jelesnianski, C. P. et al. (1992). SLOSH: Sea, Lake, and Overland Surges from Hurricanes. NOAA Technical Report NWS 48. Foundation for physical inverted barometer and wind-stress surge modeling calibrated for shallow Bay of Bengal shelf ($S_{wind} = 0.00155 \cdot V^2$).
2. Dvorak, V. F. (1984). Tropical Cyclone Intensity Analysis Using Satellite Data. NOAA Technical Report NESDIS 11. Benchmark meteorological standard for objective Dvorak pattern recognition, automated in CycloneX by our 4-channel PyTorch CNN classifier.
3. Gorelick, N. et al. (2017). Google Earth Engine: Planetary-scale geospatial analysis for everyone. Remote Sensing of Environment, 202, 18–27. Planetary computing framework powering our Sentinel-1 C-SAR flood mapping, SRTM 30m DEM, Dynamic World LULC, and VIIRS nightlights.
4. Zheng, X. et al. (2020). Deep Learning for Tropical Cyclone Intensity Estimation. IEEE TGRS, 58(8). Architectural baseline for our multi-horizon PyTorch ConvNet / LSTM track and intensity regressor (+6h, +12h, +24h, +48h).
*Every fix and finding cited in this deck is verifiable in the public commit history — github.com/MaxasOP/Cyclonex

Data Sources

1. IMD RSMC New Delhi & JTWC: rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in — official tropical cyclone track bulletins, central pressure deficits, and sustained wind observations.
2. Google Earth Engine (GEE): developers.google.com/earth-engine — Sentinel-1 SAR (COPERNICUS/S1_GRD), SRTM DEM (USGS/SRTMGL1_003), Dynamic World (GOOGLE/DYNAMICWORLD/V1), and VIIRS DNB.
3. Open-Meteo & ECMWF ERA5: open-meteo.com — high-resolution atmospheric boundary data, convective rain rates (mm/h), and surface gusts.
4. NDMA SACHET Portal: sachet.ndma.gov.in — ITU-T X.1303 CAP v1.2 early-warning alert standards & MPCS shelter criteria.

Standards, Toolings & Methods

1. IS 875 (Part 3) Indian Standard: Code of Practice for Design Loads (Wind Loads) on Buildings & Structures — engineering basis for asset vulnerability thresholds.
2. OASIS CAP v1.2 / ITU-T X.1303: Common Alerting Protocol XML schema implemented verbatim for NDMA SACHET broadcast interoperability.
3. React 19 / TypeScript / Vite / MapLibre GL: maplibre.org — browser-native WebGL vector rendering with zero GIS server license cost.
4. Three.js Engine: threejs.org — WebGL 3D digital twin rendering engine with real parcel extrusions & dynamic wind vectors.
5. FastAPI / PyTorch / Pydantic V2: fastapi.tiangolo.com, pytorch.org — async science backend serving 4 PyTorch deep learning models on CPU (<50ms).
